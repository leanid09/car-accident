# Rear suspension: how to get it covered (California)

Oct 14, 2026 · Claim CL-83-92MLUQ-5 · Third-party claim 260792281 (National General)

The Collision Center tech said the rear suspension probably won't be replaced because the insurer will say it isn't from the impact. That is a prediction, not a decision. The shop doesn't decide coverage; the insurer does, and California regulates how it decides. No law makes an insurer pay for damage the collision didn't cause, so every lever below does one of two jobs: it gets the damage measured, or it forces a written, reasoned decision you can challenge.

Not legal advice. Regulation text was checked through secondary sources because direct fetches were blocked. Confirm the wording on the [CDI Fair Claims Regulations page](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/130-laws-regs-hearings/05-CCR/fair-claims-regs.cfm) before quoting it in a letter.

## The one thing that decides this

**The pre-repair four-wheel alignment printout and the component inspection.** If right-rear camber or toe is out of spec and won't adjust back in range, or a link or arm is visibly bent, the insurer has a hard time refusing. If every reading is in spec, no legal tool will get the suspension replaced, and you should stop pushing.

## Weaknesses the adjuster will raise (fix them before they do)

1. **The truck incident.** This is their best argument. Get that claim's paperwork showing where the truck hit. If it was front or side, say so in writing. If it was rear, the August 7 claim on the suspension is mostly lost.
2. **Low delta-V.** The Model Y's own velocity change was well under the Kia's 6–9 MPH, because the contact was glancing. Use the measured body pitch at 24.0 s (`analysis/ANALYSIS.md`), not the 6–9 MPH figure alone.
3. **Side mismatch.** The deepest panel damage is on the left. The symptom is on the right rear.
4. **The common-wear explanation.** Inner-shoulder rear tire wear and suspension squeaks are widely reported on stock Model Ys with no collision involved. Expect the adjuster to say this.
5. **The front squeak.** The verified research found no mechanism linking a rear hit to the front suspension (`sources/RESEARCH.md`). **Drop the front squeak from the claim.** Keeping it costs credibility on the rear.

## The escalation ladder, in order

| Step | What you do | Legal basis | Cost / time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | In writing to the shop: alignment printout *before* any adjustment (all four wheels, plus thrust angle and setback), RR tire photos, an RR link and bushing inspection, and a supplement to the insurer if anything is found | Shop must give a written estimate and itemized invoice, and can't do or charge for unapproved work (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code 9884.8, 9884.9, enforced by the Bureau of Automotive Repair) | Free; this week |
| 2 | Email the adjuster: a verbal "not related" doesn't count, so ask for a written decision with specific reasons and the policy provision, a copy of the estimate, and what was actually inspected | 10 CCR 2695.7(b): written acceptance or denial within 40 days of proof of claim, with reasons. 2695.7(d): thorough, fair and objective investigation. 2695.8(f): a copy of the estimate | Free; 40-day clock |
| 3 | If denied: get a written estimate from an independent alignment or suspension shop, with the printout, that names the parts and says the damage is consistent with a rear impact | 2695.8(f): when your written estimate is higher, the insurer must pay the difference, name a shop that will do the work for its number, or reasonably adjust its estimate and send you the revision | $150–300 for an alignment check; 1 week |
| 4 | File a complaint with the California Department of Insurance | The insurer must respond to CDI inquiries (10 CCR 2695.5). Complaints are free and online, or 1-800-927-4357 | Free; 30–60 days |
| 5 | Go after the at-fault side: push the suspension through National General claim 260792281, and if that fails, sue the Kia driver in small claims | Up to $12,500 for an individual (CCP 116.221). No lawyers. The judge decides causation on your dashcam plus the printout. The deadline for property damage is 3 years (CCP 338) | About $75 filing fee; 2–3 months |

Skip these: the appraisal clause, because California appraisers can't decide causation (Kacha v. Allstate, 2006), and causation is the whole dispute here. Also skip a bad-faith lawsuit, which is wildly out of proportion for a repair likely to cost $500–2,500.

**Small-claims caveat.** You were backing up (CVC 22106), so a judge could put part of the fault on you, even though the telemetry shows 0 MPH at contact. Find out whether National General has accepted liability before spending time on step 5.

## Odds (speculation, labelled as such)

- Rack shows the RR out of spec and not adjustable, or a bent part is found: **~65–80%** the insurer pays after steps 1–3.
- The RR is out of spec but adjusts back in range: alignment likely paid (~50%), parts replacement unlikely (~15%).
- Everything is in spec: **under 10%**. Drop it and get a normal alignment at your own cost (about $200).
